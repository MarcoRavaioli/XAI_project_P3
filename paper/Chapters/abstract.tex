We train Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) on the MLP output activations of a pre-trained ViT-B/16 at four network depths, and use CLIP as an external cross-modal evaluator, with an uncertainty-abstention margin, to assign interpretable labels to discovered sparse directions in a purely visual model. Across depth we observe a coherent progression: early features (Layer~2) are uncertain texture detectors, a mid layer (Layer~9) is net-inhibitory, and late layers (Layers~10--11) concentrate positive causal weight on cleanly monosemantic object features (e.g.\ \textit{American lobster}: $+0.56\%$ relative logit drop, 95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.17,+1.04]$). All causal effects are estimated over a held-out evaluation set with bootstrap confidence intervals rather than single images. Repeating the identical pipeline on a self-supervised DINOv2-B backbone reveals a sharp contrast: although a linear probe on its embeddings reaches $99.5\%$ accuracy, the matched-recipe SAEs collapse to dead latents at every depth except one, showing that the supervised-ViT SAE recipe does not transfer without per-backbone re-tuning. Overall, the study provides a strong SAE-based layer-wise characterisation for supervised ViTs and quantifies limits of cross-backbone recipe transfer; explicit tracing of patch-to-\texttt{[CLS]} aggregation is left for future circuit-level work.
