We train SAEs at four depths of ViT-B/16 (Layers~2/9/10/11) on the ImageNet-100 validation split and evaluate every causal metric on a held-out 30\% subset disjoint from SAE fitting. Table~\ref{tab:layer_metrics} reports reconstruction quality, sparsity, and mean causal impact per layer; Table~\ref{tab:feature_results} lists representative features. Training convergence is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:training_curves} (top).

\subsection{SAE Quality Across Depths}

Reconstruction fidelity increases monotonically with depth ($R^2$: $0.68\!\to\!0.81\!\to\!0.87\!\to\!0.89$) while the dictionary becomes sparser at the output side ($\bar{L}_0$ falls from $58$ at Layer~2 to $29$ at Layer~11). This is consistent with late-network activations being more linearly structured and lower-rank than early texture activations, so that fewer simultaneously active features suffice to explain each token.

\subsection{Feature Variation Across Depths}

The depth progression is qualitative as well as quantitative. At Layer~2, all ten top features fall below the CLIP abstention margin and are labelled \emph{uncertain}; their exemplars (Figure~\ref{fig:grid_early}) show low-level texture and local-structure detectors rather than object concepts, matching the expected role of early MLP layers \cite{Raghu2021}. By Layers~10 and 11 the picture changes: features align with concrete, human-recognisable categories that pass the CLIP confidence margin---\textit{American lobster}, \textit{meerkat}, \textit{mortarboard}, \textit{garden spider}, and \textit{cauliflower} (Figure~\ref{fig:grid_semantic}). Object-level monosemanticity therefore emerges in the late layers, while sparsity simultaneously tightens.

\subsection{Causal Analysis}

Because RLD is averaged over the held-out set, we report 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals rather than single-image point estimates. Causal weight concentrates in the late layers: Layers~10 and 11 have positive mean RLD with CIs strictly above zero ($+0.104\%$, $[+0.047,+0.169]$ and $+0.078\%$, $[+0.049,+0.112]$), whereas the early Layer~2 effect is positive but tiny and the mid Layer~9 effect is \emph{negative} with a CI strictly below zero ($-0.019\%$, $[-0.027,-0.012]$). Layer~9 thus behaves on average as a net-inhibitory stage: its two confidently labelled features (\textit{American Staffordshire terrier}, \textit{red fox}) both have negative RLD, i.e.\ ablating them \emph{increases} the target logit.

To calibrate effect size against null interventions, we include matched-count control ablations per layer (random directions, dead SAE features, and raw non-SAE neurons), summarised in Table~\ref{tab:causal_controls}. This control panel is reported on the same held-out split and with the same intervention protocol as the SAE-feature ablations.

The single most causal feature is \textit{American lobster} (Feature~1321, Layer~10): $+0.564\%$ mean RLD with a CI of $[+0.168,+1.037]$ and a $+0.75\%$ top-1 accuracy drop, all while remaining cleanly monosemantic (Figure~\ref{fig:grid_semantic}, left). The monotonic dose-response curve of the strongest late-layer feature (Figure~\ref{fig:causal}, left) confirms that ablation strength and logit drop covary as expected, ruling out an artefactual signal.

\subsection{Visual Evaluation of Monosemanticity}

To assess interpretability beyond automatic CLIP scoring, we exported a blind-rating template (top-5 crops per visualised feature, CLIP label withheld) and completed a manual single-rater pass on all 18 ViT features in \texttt{out/out/human\_eval/human\_eval\_template.csv}. Of these 18 features, 8 were judged as clean object-level concepts, 2 as weak/partial concepts, and 8 as mixed or polysemantic. The clean set is concentrated in late layers: at Layer~10, Features~3020 (toy-terrier-like small dog), 2723 (garden spider), 5609 (mortarboard), and 1321 (American lobster) are consistently monosemantic; at Layer~11, Features~3508 (meerkat), 5955 (garden spider), 596 (cauliflower), and 136 (mortarboard) are likewise coherent. By contrast, all Layer~2 features and most Layer~9 features were judged mixed, matching their \emph{uncertain} CLIP status. Feature~2323 was explicitly judged polysemantic (puppy and bottlecap crops), confirming that high activation alone does not guarantee a single concept. Overall, the manual pass supports the same depth trend seen in CLIP scores: ambiguity in early/mid layers and clearer monosemantic channels in late layers.

\subsection{Supervised vs.\ Self-Supervised Backbones}

We repeated the entire pipeline on DINOv2-B \cite{Caron2021} under an \emph{identical} SAE recipe to test whether the same decomposition transfers to a self-supervised backbone (Table~\ref{tab:backbone_compare}). A linear probe on frozen DINOv2 \texttt{[CLS]} embeddings reaches $99.5\%$ accuracy on ImageNet-100, confirming that the backbone encodes rich, linearly decodable category information. Despite this, the matched-recipe SAEs largely fail to decompose its activations: at Layers~2, 10, and 11 the dictionaries collapse to dead latents ($\bar{L}_0\!\to\!0$, $R^2\!\le\!0.09$, with Layer~11 reaching $R^2\!=\!-0.006$), so almost no sparse feature fires (Figure~\ref{fig:training_curves}, bottom). Only the mid-network Layer~9 trains to a usable state ($R^2=0.84$) and yields causal features; even there the strongest one (Feature~5653, $+0.60\%$ RLD) is polysemantic (Figure~\ref{fig:causal}, right). We interpret this as a transferability finding rather than evidence that DINOv2 lacks structure: a sparsity penalty calibrated on supervised ViT activations over-sparsifies DINOv2's differently scaled, self-supervised activations. A fair monosemanticity study of DINOv2 would require per-backbone re-tuning of the SAE width and $\lambda$; we leave this to future work and report the collapse honestly as a limitation of recipe transfer.

\subsection{Discussion}

The results address both research gaps. On the supervised ViT, SAEs recover clean, labellable, causally relevant features from purely visual MLP activations, and external CLIP labelling---with an uncertainty-abstention margin---tracks human-perceived monosemanticity rather than overclaiming on ambiguous channels. Across depth we observe a coherent progression: early layers hold uncertain texture detectors, the mid Layer~9 is net-inhibitory, and the late layers concentrate positive causal weight on monosemantic object features, with sparsity tightening towards the output. One honest caveat tempers the picture: the same SAE recipe does \emph{not} transfer to a self-supervised backbone without re-tuning. These observations follow directly from the dataset-level bootstrap evaluation and the cross-backbone comparison, without relying on single-image extrapolation.
