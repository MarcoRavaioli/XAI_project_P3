\subsection{Mechanistic Interpretability in Transformers}
Elhage et al.\ \cite{Elhage2021} formalised the residual stream framework: attention heads route information between tokens while MLP blocks process within-token representations. Building on this, Conmy et al.\ \cite{Conmy2023} introduced ACDC, which identifies the minimal computational subgraph responsible for a target behaviour via iterative activation patching. SAEs applied to MLP activations or residual stream vectors recover a larger dictionary of sparse, monosemantic features that influence model predictions in interpretable ways \cite{Cunningham2024, Bricken2023}. These contributions target language models exclusively.

\subsection{ViTs and Concept Discovery in Vision-Language Models}
Raghu et al.\ \cite{Raghu2021} showed that ViTs aggregate global information early via self-attention and propagate features strongly through residual connections---properties that complicate circuit-level analysis relative to language models. In the vision-language domain, Gandelsman et al.\ \cite{Gandelsman2024} decompose CLIP image representations as sparse sums of text-direction embeddings; Haque et al.\ \cite{Haque2026} apply SAEs to medical VLMs and anchor discovered concepts to clinical ontologies. Both approaches rest on pre-existing text-image alignment unavailable in purely supervised ViTs.
